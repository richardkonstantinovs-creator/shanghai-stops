How the scale works

Every place on this site carries one number: how many metro stops it is worth riding for. Nought to fifteen. That is the whole system.

Why stops and not stars

A star rating answers "is this good", which is not the question anyone standing on a platform is actually asking. The question is is this worth the journey, and the honest unit for that is the journey.

A Shanghai metro stop is about 1.2 km and about two and a half minutes. Fifteen stops is most of the way across the city. So a twelve means: get on a train, change lines, ride for the better part of forty minutes, and you will still be glad you did.

It also means a small, cheap, unglamorous place can beat a famous one, which is the point. Nothing here is scored on how impressive it is.

The five bands

0 Don't
I went so you don't have to.
1–3 If you're there
Worth a detour of one street, not one stop.
4–7 Worth the ride
A normal, good reason to get on a train.
8–11 Cross the city
Change lines for this. Twice, if you have to.
12–15 Last supper
If you had one meal left in Shanghai.

Nought is a verdict, not a distance

A nought does not mean "we did not like it much". It means we went, we paid, and we would tell a friend not to. Those entries are here in full, free, with the reason — a guide that never rejects anything is not believed. All of them are on one page.

Negative verdicts stay personal. "It wasn't for me and I wouldn't go back" is the strongest thing you will read here. We live in this city and accusations about a business's hygiene or honesty are taken seriously in it, so we do not make them.

That is a separate claim from a high score. It means: worth riding to straight off a long-haul flight, jet-lagged, with a suitcase, before you have even checked in. There are not many.

Nerve

Every place also carries a nerve score from one to five. It is not about danger — it is about how much of yourself you have to spend to walk in. Language, strangeness, whether anyone will help you.

1 easy
English on the menu, or pointing is enough.
2 mild
No English, but the food is visible and the staff are used to it.
3 real
You will have to order by characters or by luck.
4 hard
No English, no pictures, and nobody has time to help you.
5 brutal
You will be stared at, and you will not be sure what you ate.

The words are the scale, not the numbers. They have not changed since the first record was written and they will not, because changing one of them would silently re-read every score already given. Everything at 1 or 2 is here if you want somewhere to start.

How a place gets chosen

Not by searching for it. A separate app picks one at random out of every venue in Shanghai that OpenStreetMap knows about, filtered only by distance and by what kind of place it is. Whatever it lands on is where we go, and it is scored afterwards whether it was any good or not.

A place already visited is never selected again, so nothing on this site was chosen because we expected to like it. That is where the noughts come from, and it is the only reason the twelves mean anything.

Prices

Every price on this site carries the date it was checked. A price with no date is worse than no price. If a price is more than nine months old the build says so, and if an order card here is stale it says so on the card itself, in red, rather than quietly.
