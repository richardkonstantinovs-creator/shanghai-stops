About

Two brothers. One lives in Shanghai and does the walking. One is in the Netherlands and does the data, the writing and the site.

How a place ends up here

An app picks one at random from every venue in Shanghai that OpenStreetMap has, filtered only by how far away it is and what kind of place it is. Whatever it lands on, he walks there and films the walk. He scores it afterwards, in the app, on the spot.

He does not get to choose and he does not get to skip. That is why 2 of the places on this site are marked "don't" — they came up, we went, and they were not worth it. The scale is explained here.

What is never sponsored

No place score, verdict or order card is ever sponsored or affiliated, ever.

Not discounted, not comped, not arranged in advance. Nobody on this site knows they are on it. We pay for every meal on it and the price you see is the price we paid, on the date printed beside it.

We are not vague about this because vagueness is where it goes wrong. A "nothing is sponsored" that turns out to have exceptions costs more than any of it was worth.

Where money does come from

  • Routes and guides. Days in the city put in order, with the connections, the timings and the fallbacks. That is the only thing sold here. They are here.
  • Affiliate links, in one place only. The eSIM and travel-tool guide carries them, disclosed at the top of that page rather than in a footer. If you buy through one we are paid a commission at no extra cost to you.

Everything else is free and stays free: every place page in full, every order card, every skip-it entry, the map, and the scale. The line is between information and a plan. Information is free; the plan is the product.

What we get wrong

Prices drift. Shops close. A woman with a folding table is not a business with opening hours. If something here is wrong, tell us — hello@stops.example — and it gets fixed, or the page gets a line saying it was wrong.

Place coordinates come from OpenStreetMap and are © OpenStreetMap contributors, used under the ODbL. Every place page carries both a WGS-84 and a GCJ-02 pair, because Chinese map apps are offset from satellite coordinates by three to six hundred metres here and giving you only one would send you to the wrong street.

Six emails, timed to your trip

Tell us when you fly and you get six short emails on a schedule: six weeks out, four weeks, two weeks, one week, three days, and the night before. Each one is a single thing to do at the point it is actually useful — the payment app while you still have time to fix it, the eSIM before you are in a taxi queue, the two things to screenshot the night before.

Then it stops. It is a pre-trip sequence, not a newsletter. When your trip arrives, so does the last one.

We send a confirmation link first and nothing else until you click it. Every message has an unsubscribe link in it. We store your address and your departure date, nothing else, and we delete both thirty days after the last email goes out. No tracking pixels, no sharing, no list rental.
