Practical guides

The metro, which is the whole point

Twenty lines, about four yuan a ride, English on every sign. How to pay for it and how to read it.

Verified on 2026-08-24 by Richard, in the Netherlands, in Shanghai, by doing it.

This site measures things in metro stops, so it is worth being clear about what a stop is.

What it costs

Fares are distance-based and start around three yuan, rising to about nine or ten for a very long ride across the city. A normal journey on this site — five or six stops — is four or five yuan. That is under sixty cents.

Trains run from roughly 05:30 to 22:30. The last train is earlier than you think and it is the single most common way a good evening ends badly. Check the last-train time at the station you will be coming back from, not the one you are leaving from.

How to pay

Three ways, in the order I would try them:

  1. The QR code in Alipay. Open the Transport section, add the Shanghai metro card, scan the code at the gate. No deposit, no queue, no card to lose. This is what I use. See paying for things.
  2. A physical transport card, bought and topped up at a station machine. Works on the metro, the buses and the ferries. Takes cash. Worth it if there are several of you.
  3. A single-journey ticket from the machine at the station. Fine once, tedious by the fourth time.

Reading a station

Every sign is bilingual and every announcement is in Mandarin and English. You will not get lost on the train. You will get lost at the exit, which is why every place on this site carries an exit number.

A big interchange can have fifteen exits spread over half a kilometre. Coming out of the wrong one at People’s Square puts you ten minutes and one large road away from where you meant to be. Look for the exit number on the platform signs before you go up the escalator, not after.

Security

Every station has a bag scanner at the entrance. It takes about fifteen seconds and everyone does it. Have your bag off your shoulder before you reach it.

Rush hour

Between about 07:30 and 09:30, and again from 17:30 to 19:30, Lines 1, 2 and 8 are genuinely full — the kind of full where you wait for the second train. Almost everything on this site is timed to avoid it, and that is not politeness, it is because you cannot enjoy anything you are about to eat after twenty minutes of that.
